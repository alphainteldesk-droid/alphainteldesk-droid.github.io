What are prediction markets? A plain-English guide

A prediction market lets you trade on what will happen next: an election, a Fed decision, a price, a world event. Here's how it works in two minutes, with a picture.

Updated November 12, 2026 · 3 min read

The short version

Every market is a yes-or-no question. You buy Yes or No shares priced between 1¢ and 99¢. If you're right, each share pays $1. If you're wrong, it pays nothing.

The price is the crowd's odds: 30¢ ≈ a 30% chance.

How it works, in four steps

1

A question

"Will the Fed cut rates in December?"

Yes or No
2

You pick a side

Buy Yes or No. The price shows the crowd's odds.

Yes = 30¢ → ~30% chance
3

The price moves

News breaks, traders buy and sell. You can sell any time before the end.

30¢ → 55¢
4

It settles

The answer is known. The right side gets $1 a share, the wrong side $0.

Yes ✓ → $1.00

What you can win or lose

Say you buy 10 Yes shares at 30¢. That costs $3.

Before fees. Each site charges a small trading fee; our Gap Scanner fee table shows them side by side. You never lose more than you paid for your shares.

Why the price means something

Traders have real money on the line, so they're rewarded for being right, not for being loud. When news changes the odds, people buy or sell and the price moves within minutes. That's why prediction markets are often watched as a fast, live forecast.

The price isn't a guarantee. Markets can be wrong, thin markets can be pushed around by a few trades, and the same question can trade at different prices on different sites.

Four things to know before you trade

  • Read the rules. Each market says exactly what counts as Yes, by when, and who decides (for example AP or a government report). Two markets with the same title can have different rules.
  • Fees. Most sites charge a small fee when you buy straight away. It's largest near 50¢.
  • Availability. Each site decides who can join. Check it's available where you live.
  • Risk. Only trade what you can afford to lose.

FAQ

Is a prediction market the same as betting?

You're trading shares with other people, not betting against a bookmaker, and you can sell before the end. Some sites are regulated as financial exchanges. Rules differ by country and state.

Can I sell before the market ends?

Yes. You can sell your shares at the current price any time the market is open, for a profit or a loss.

Who decides the answer?

Each market names its source in the rules, such as an official data release or a major news outlet.
