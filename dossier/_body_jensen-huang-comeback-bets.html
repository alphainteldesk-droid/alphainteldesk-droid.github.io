Somebody Knew: The Jensen Huang Comeback Bets

Brand-new wallets bet on Jensen Huang's return to Nvidia days before anyone said it out loud, at odds of 2 to 6 cents. We can't prove who they are, but on the face of the data, someone knew something.

On Nov 17, Nvidia's chair told investors the board "does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO." Six days later, before Monday's opening bell, it did exactly that. In between, a handful of Polymarket accounts that had barely existed a week earlier bought thousands of dollars of "yes, he comes back" at prices that said the market thought it almost couldn't happen.

We pulled every public trade on Polymarket's "Jensen Huang rejoins NVIDIA" contracts, the Kalshi trade tape and the Alpha Arcade blockchain. Here's what the records show.

The timeline that matters

Huang resigned on Nov 12, after admitting an affair with a woman U.S. officials suspect of spying for China. According to CNBC, White House officials contacted the board overnight Friday, Nov 20. Huang met the board Saturday and again Sunday, when it decided to bring him back. CNN reports the board told officials that night. President Trump posted "I hope the Board of Nvidia rehires Jensen Huang" late Sunday, and Nvidia announced the reinstatement just before 9 AM ET Monday. The stock jumped about 31%.

So the decision existed for hours, inside a small circle, before the public knew. That's exactly when an insider would trade.

What the wallets did

From Thursday evening, Nov 19, to the announcement, more than $80,000 of "yes" was bought on Polymarket's four return contracts. Most came from established accounts. But among the 50 biggest buyers, 10 wallets were less than two weeks old. Together they spent about $11,700 on roughly 225,000 shares, which paid out about $225,000 when Huang came back.

Five of them started buying before Trump's Sunday-night post, before any public sign that the board had changed its mind:

  • A wallet first used on Nov 16, which has only ever traded Nvidia markets, bought 77,311 "yes" shares at about 2¢ on Friday, Nov 20. Cost: $1,459. Payout: about $77,000.
  • An account named "jensencmback" made its first-ever trade on Thursday evening, Nov 19: 20,000 shares of "back by Dec 31" at 5.6¢.
  • Another wallet, nine days old and active only in Nvidia markets, spent $2,429 on Friday on the longer "by end of 2027" contract.

Then, 34 minutes before the announcement, an 11-day-old wallet bought 30,658 shares of "back by Nov 30" at 3.1¢. That's a $948 bet on something the market priced at 3%, paying $30,658 half an hour later.

The blockchain firm Bubblemaps found the same pattern independently: two clusters of 16 new accounts that turned about $20,000 into about $350,000, with 11 of them funded on Monday.

What the other two sites show

Kalshi hides who trades, but its tape is public. In the four days before the news, only about $1,300 of "yes" changed hands on its "Huang joins Nvidia" contract. The big buys came after the announcement. Alpha Arcade was quiet too: every wallet in its Huang return market was months or years old, and nobody bought the cheap "by Nov 30" contract until after the news. If someone knew, they went where the liquidity was: Polymarket.

Why it matters

This isn't a free-for-all. The CFTC brought its first insider-trading case on event contracts in April, and its enforcement chief has called the idea that insider rules don't apply to prediction markets a myth. Polymarket's own rules bar trades on stolen confidential information or illegal tips. A board decision that moved Nvidia's stock 31% in a day is about as confidential as information gets.

The best argument against us

The return wasn't a secret wish. Huang said on Nov 17 he'd be honored to come back, Trump had said he wouldn't mind, and employees and Elon Musk were campaigning for it. Cheap long shots attract gamblers, and a 2¢ bet on a famous comeback is the kind of ticket people buy for fun. Much of the Monday-morning buying came after Trump's post, which was public. And Bubblemaps itself says it found no on-chain link between the accounts. Timing is evidence, not proof.

The Desk's Take

OPINION

The proof is in the pudding. On the face of it, from the data we have, someone knew something. Luck doesn't open a fresh wallet, trade nothing but Nvidia, and load up at 2¢ the same weekend a board quietly changes its mind. One account like that is a gambler. A cluster of them, buying before the public had any reason to, is a pattern. Polymarket's records are public and permanent, and these bets will be hard to explain.
