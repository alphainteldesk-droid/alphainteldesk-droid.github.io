Why Trump Won't Fire Lutnick

Trump's Commerce Secretary is under fire from his own party over an Nvidia spy scandal, and markets put his chance of being out by March 31 at 37–45%. But Trump keeps backing him, and from January 3 a Democratic Senate would have to confirm any replacement. Our view: he stays.

Who he is

  • Born July 14, 1961 in Jericho, New York. Graduated from Haverford College in 1983 and joined Cantor Fitzgerald the same year.
  • Ran Cantor Fitzgerald from age 29 (president and CEO in 1991, chairman by 1996), one of Wall Street's longest-serving CEOs. He also led BGC Group and Newmark. His two oldest sons now run Cantor.
  • Has known Trump for years, from Manhattan's charity circuit, and appeared alongside him on The Celebrity Apprentice. Raised about $75 million for Trump's 2024 campaign and co-chaired the transition.
  • Confirmed as Commerce Secretary on Feb 18, 2025, by 51–45. Commerce runs tariffs and trade enforcement and, crucially for this story, the Entity List of companies barred from buying sensitive US technology.
  • Reported at least $250 million in income and proceeds for 2025 on his annual disclosure, much of it from the businesses he left behind.
  • On X: @howardlutnick.

September 11: the day he wasn't there

Cantor Fitzgerald's offices were on floors 101–105 of the North Tower. On September 11, 2001, Lutnick was late because it was his son Kyle's first day of kindergarten, and he walked him to the classroom. Flight 11 hit below Cantor's floors at 8:46 AM. 658 of the firm's roughly 960 New York employees died, including his younger brother Gary and his best friend Doug Gardner. No one in the office that morning survived.

Within days the firm set up a relief fund and pledged a quarter of its profits for five years to the victims' families, and Lutnick rebuilt the company, though some of his hard-nosed decisions in the weeks after drew criticism. Dozens of the victims' children have since worked at Cantor.

Why he's in trouble now

  1. Feb 10, 2026

    At a Senate hearing, Lutnick acknowledges a 2012 family lunch on Jeffrey Epstein's island, years after he said he had cut off contact in 2005. Newly released files show contact through 2018. The White House stands by him.

  2. Nov 3

    Democrats win both the House and the Senate in the midterms. The new Senate is sworn in Jan 3, 2027.

  3. Nov 5

    Axios reports Bessent confronted Lutnick in the Situation Room over why Megaspeed, a Singapore firm suspected of routing Nvidia chips to China, was never added to the Entity List. Commerce denies the exchange happened.

  4. Nov 6

    Trump replaces chief of staff Susie Wiles with Stephen Miller, days after the midterm losses.

  5. Nov 12

    Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigns after an affair with Alice Huang, Megaspeed's former head and a suspected Chinese spy. Reuters reports senior White House officials are discussing whether Lutnick should be pushed out.

  6. Nov 13

    Lutnick agrees to testify to the Senate Banking Committee on Dec 10. Bessent tells CNBC four agencies wanted Megaspeed listed for months, but "Commerce has the pen."

  7. Nov 16

    Asked twice whether he'll replace Lutnick, Trump says no, and that he's done a very good job. He suggests any delay on Megaspeed was to avoid a blowup with China. Lutnick joins Trump's Asia trip.

  8. Nov 21

    The Wall Street Journal reports Trump came close to firing Lutnick earlier this year over the Epstein visit, then stopped talking about it after Lutnick gave $25 million to his presidential library, a gift first reported in the book Regime Change.

  9. Dec 10

    Senate Banking Committee testimony on Megaspeed and chip exports.

  10. Jan 3, 2027

    Democratic Senate takes over. Any new Commerce Secretary now needs Democratic votes.

The case he's out

  • Pressure is bipartisan: Republican senators, including Banking Committee member Jim Banks, say Commerce appears to have used loopholes to allow chip sales despite Trump's directive.
  • Bessent, one of Trump's most trusted officials, is publicly pinning Megaspeed on Commerce, and Axios reports Lutnick intervened against export actions at least three times this year.
  • Reuters reports senior White House officials have discussed pushing him out.
  • The Epstein history hasn't gone away, and from January a Democratic House can subpoena.
  • The Dec 10 hearing is a live risk: one bad answer under oath could change everything.

The case he stays

  • Asked twice on Nov 16, Trump said "no" both times and praised his work. He even defended the Megaspeed delay as keeping the peace with China.
  • The White House says Trump "maintains complete confidence" in him.
  • He has already survived one near-firing (Epstein, earlier this year) and, per the WSJ, a $25 million gift followed.
  • After Jan 3, replacing him means a confirmation fight in a Democratic Senate, or months with an acting secretary.

The Desk's Take

OPINION

His ties are too deep. Lutnick isn't a Cabinet hire Trump met at an interview. He's a friend of many years who raised $75 million for him, ran his transition, and (if the Journal is right) wrote a $25 million check the last time his job was in doubt. Presidents fire people who embarrass them and have nothing left to offer. Lutnick still has plenty to offer, and Trump said as much in public this month.

The real test is the relationship, not the scandal. The question isn't whether Republicans are angry. They are. The question is whether Trump personally decides Lutnick has become a liability to him. Megaspeed doesn't get there on its own: Trump has already framed the delay as his own China strategy.

What would it take? Something that turns this from Lutnick's problem into Trump's problem. Proof that Lutnick went against a direct Trump order, not just against Bessent. A disastrous Dec 10 hearing. An investigation that lands on Commerce itself. Or the China deal falling apart, which would kill the "avoid a blowup" defense. Short of that, the math favors staying: firing him now means handing a Democratic Senate a confirmation fight over Commerce in 2027.

What to watch

  • Dec 10: Senate Banking Committee testimony. Watch for any sign he defied a Trump directive.
