Why Trump Won't Fire Lutnick

The Nvidia spy scandal has Washington writing Howard Lutnick's exit. Markets give him a 37–45% chance of being gone by March 31. We think they're wrong: his ties to Trump run too deep, and he stays.

Every week brings a new reason Howard Lutnick should be finished. The Commerce Secretary is accused of sitting on a decision to blacklist Megaspeed, a Singapore firm suspected of routing Nvidia chips to China. The scandal has already cost Nvidia's CEO, Jensen Huang, his job. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent says four agencies wanted Megaspeed listed for months but "Commerce has the pen." Republican senators are angry, Reuters reports senior White House officials have discussed pushing Lutnick out, and he has to testify to the Senate Banking Committee on Dec 10.

On paper, that's a man on his way out. But this story only turns on one thing: Trump. And Trump doesn't fire friends like this.

This isn't a normal Cabinet hire

Lutnick has known Trump for years, from Manhattan's charity circuit to a turn on The Celebrity Apprentice. He raised about $75 million for the 2024 campaign and co-chaired the transition. And according to the Wall Street Journal, the last time his job was in doubt, over a 2012 visit to Jeffrey Epstein's island, Trump stopped talking about firing him after Lutnick gave $25 million to his presidential library.

He's also a survivor in the truest sense. On Sept 11, 2001, he was late to Cantor Fitzgerald's offices atop the North Tower because he was walking his son to his first day of kindergarten. 658 of the firm's New York employees died, his brother among them. He rebuilt the company anyway. People who've come back from that don't fold at a Senate hearing.

Trump has already answered the question

Asked twice on Nov 16 whether he'd replace Lutnick, Trump said no, and praised his work. More telling, he defended the Megaspeed delay as a way to avoid a blowup with China. That turns Lutnick's biggest problem into Trump's own strategy. You don't fire a man for carrying out your plan.

Then there's the calendar. Democrats won the Senate on Nov 3, and the new Senate is sworn in Jan 3. After that, any new Commerce Secretary needs Democratic votes: a long, ugly confirmation fight over trade and China, or months with an acting secretary. Firing Lutnick doesn't end the story for Trump. It starts a worse one.

What the market says

Polymarket prices "Lutnick out by March 31, 2027" at 37%, Kalshi at 45.5%. Both fell sharply after Trump backed him on Nov 16. Read the rules carefully: Polymarket counts an announced exit, while Kalshi needs him to actually leave. That makes Polymarket's version easier to win as YES, yet it's the cheaper one. The best argument against us

This could stop being Lutnick's problem and become Trump's. If the Dec 10 hearing shows Lutnick defied a direct Trump order, not just Bessent, or if the China truce collapses and takes the "avoid a blowup" defense with it, Trump has every reason to cut him loose. If that happens, it happens fast, and before Jan 3, while Republicans still control confirmations. A quiet December is the tell that we're right.

The Desk's Take

OPINION

His ties are too deep. Presidents fire people who embarrass them and have nothing left to offer. Lutnick still has plenty to offer, Trump said so in public this month, and he has already survived one near-firing. Short of hard proof that he went against Trump himself, he stays.
