Kalshi vs Polymarket vs Alpha Arcade: which prediction market fits you?

Three prediction markets, three very different setups. Here's how they compare on how they work, what they cost, how you pay, and what you can trade, in plain English.

Updated November 4, 2026 · 6 min read

The short version

Kalshi: a US-regulated exchange where you trade in dollars. Biggest for sports and economic events.

Polymarket: the world's biggest prediction market. US traders use the separate, US-regulated Polymarket US.

Alpha Arcade: a newer, smaller market on the Algorand blockchain. Trades in USDC, lets the community create markets, and pays rewards to liquidity providers.

How prediction markets work (30-second refresher)

Every market is a yes-or-no question, like "Will the Fed cut rates in December?" You buy Yes or No shares priced between 1¢ and 99¢. If you're right, each share pays $1. If you're wrong, it pays nothing. The price works like the crowd's odds: a Yes price of 62¢ means traders put the chance at about 62%. All three platforms work this way. The differences are in the details below.

Kalshi

KALSHI

Kalshi is a US company and a CFTC-regulated exchange, the same kind of oversight that covers futures markets. You open an account like a brokerage account, fund it in dollars by bank transfer, and trade without touching crypto.

Fees

Kalshi charges a fee when your order trades instantly against someone else's (a "taker" order). The fee is largest for contracts priced around 50¢, about 1.75¢ per contract at that price, and shrinks as prices move toward 1¢ or 99¢. Orders that wait on the book for someone to take them ("maker" orders) pay a much smaller fee.

Good to know

  • Sports make up most of Kalshi's trading, but it also lists Fed decisions, inflation and jobs reports, weather, politics and culture.
  • Kalshi doesn't show who is trading, so you can't follow specific traders there.

Polymarket

POLYMARKET

Polymarket is the largest prediction market in the world, with the widest range of topics: elections, world events, crypto, sports and pop culture. There are now two versions:

  • Polymarket (international): runs on crypto. You trade with USDC from a crypto wallet.
  • Polymarket US: a separate, CFTC-regulated exchange for US traders. It launched in December 2025, uses US dollars, and needs no crypto wallet.

Fees

On Polymarket US, orders that wait on the book pay no fee. Orders that trade instantly pay a fee that depends on the category (for example, more on crypto markets, less on politics) and, like Kalshi, is largest around 50¢.

Good to know

  • On the international site, every trade is on a public blockchain, so big wallets ("whales") can be tracked.
  • The biggest markets attract millions of dollars in trading, so prices are usually tight on popular questions.

Alpha Arcade

ALPHA ARCADE

Alpha Arcade is a prediction market built on Algorand, a fast, low-cost blockchain. Each market is a smart contract: Yes and No shares are tokens, your money is held in USDC, and winning shares pay out $1 each in USDC when the market resolves. It uses an order book with limit orders, like the bigger platforms.

Why Algorand matters

Kalshi is a traditional, company-run exchange. Polymarket settles trades on a blockchain but matches orders on its own servers. Alpha Arcade runs on Algorand ($ALGO), a decentralized public blockchain, from the ground up, and that brings real advantages:

  • Instant finality: a block settles in about 3 seconds, and once your trade is in, it's final. No waiting for confirmations, no chain splits.
  • Never gone down: Algorand has run without a network outage since its mainnet launched in June 2019.
  • Built for the quantum era: Algorand is a leader in post-quantum security. It has protected its history with quantum-resistant signatures since 2022, added quantum-resistant accounts in 2026, and is working toward full coverage by 2027.
  • Tiny, steady fees: network fees are a fraction of a cent and don't spike when the network gets busy.
  • Open and checkable: markets and payouts are smart contracts on a public chain, so anyone can verify them.

Getting started

You don't need to own crypto first. Alpha Arcade lets you deposit by card through Coinbase, and if you already have USDC on Algorand you can send it straight in.

What makes it different

  • Community markets: users can propose and launch their own markets.
  • Liquidity rewards: some markets pay rewards to traders who keep buy and sell orders on the book, which suits patient traders who like to post limit orders.
  • Less crowded: it's much smaller than Kalshi or Polymarket. That means fewer big players, but also thinner order books, so check the spread before you trade.
  • Public trades: like Polymarket's main site, trades are on-chain and can be followed.

FAQ

Is this legal where I live?

Each platform sets its own rules about who can join, and those rules change. Check the platform's own site to confirm it's available where you live before signing up.

Do I need crypto?

Not for Kalshi or Polymarket US, which use dollars. Polymarket's international site and Alpha Arcade use USDC, though Alpha Arcade lets you deposit with a card through Coinbase.

Which has the lowest fees?

It depends on how you trade. On both Kalshi and Polymarket US, fees are highest near 50¢, and orders that wait on the book pay less (or nothing on Polymarket US). Compare the current fee pages for the markets you actually trade.

Why does Alpha Arcade run on Algorand?

Algorand gives it instant finality, tiny fixed fees, a spotless uptime record since 2019 and leading post-quantum security. Markets and payouts are smart contracts anyone can verify.

Can I see what big traders are doing?

On Polymarket and Alpha Arcade, yes: trades are public on the blockchain. Kalshi keeps traders private, so there you can only see large trades, not who made them.
