How to read it: green is the lowest price for that outcome, red the highest. The gap is the difference in cents per $1 share, minus each site's trading fee, shown site by site under every gap (how we calculate it). Prices are midpoints, so the bid/ask spread can shrink a gap further. Vol is the total traded on each site's market, 24h the last day (Polymarket and Alpha Arcade in dollars, Kalshi in contracts). Low volume can mean a price is hard to trade at. Matches are automatic. Always check each site's rules before you trade.

How the scanner picks gaps

All three sites first, true matches only

Gaps priced on all three sites (Polymarket, Kalshi and Alpha Arcade) are listed first. Below them come gaps between two sites. Every market has to match: same event, same outcome, same end date and no rule warnings. "Similar" markets never count.

Real trading behind it

We skip markets with almost no trading, prices under 3¢ or over 97¢, markets about to close, and gaps so large they usually mean a stale or mismatched market.

Three markets, one screen

Kalshi, Polymarket and Alpha Arcade side by side. Other gap trackers skip Alpha Arcade. We don't.

After fees, not before

A 6¢ difference can shrink to 2¢, or to nothing, once each platform takes its fee. We do that math for you, using each site's published fee formula for that kind of market, and show each fee under every gap. See the formula.

Rules checked

Two markets can look identical but settle differently. We compare the end date and cutoff, the number in the question (for example "above 4%" vs "above 5%"), US vs worldwide, and draw and overtime rules. Any difference keeps a pair out of the gap math. We don't yet compare each site's data source or what happens if a market is cancelled, so always read both sites' rules.

How we calculate the gap after fees

All three sites use the same kind of fee formula. You pay it when your order fills against one already waiting on the book (a "taker" order). It's biggest at 50¢ and shrinks toward 1¢ and 99¢:

fee per $1 share = rate × price × (1 − price)

Worked example

  1. The same question trades at 34.5¢ on Polymarket (a politics market) and 39.5¢ on Kalshi. The price gap is 5.0¢.
  2. Polymarket fee at 34.5¢: 4% × 0.345 × 0.655 = 0.9¢.
  3. Kalshi fee at 39.5¢: 7% × 0.395 × 0.605 = 1.7¢.
  4. Gap after fees: 5.0 − 0.9 − 1.7 = 2.4¢ per $1 share. That's the number shown on the scanner.

Why both fees? Using a gap means trading on both sites: Yes where it's cheap, No where Yes is expensive. Together those pay $1 whatever happens, so a gap is only worth what's left after both sites take their fee. Gaps under 1¢ after fees aren't shown.

What the number doesn't include yet

  • The spread. We use the midpoint price. The price you can actually buy at is usually a little higher, and the price you can sell at a little lower.
  • How much you can trade. A thin order book may only hold a few dollars at that price.
  • Rounding on small orders. Kalshi rounds each order's fee up to the next cent, so 1-2 contract orders pay more.
  • Deposits and waiting. Card deposit fees, network costs, and money tied up until the market settles.
  • Limit orders. Orders that wait on the book usually pay no fee, so the real cost can be lower than shown.

What is prediction market arbitrage?

Arbitrage means buying the same thing in two places at different prices. In prediction markets, it usually means buying Yes on one platform and No on another when the two prices add up to less than $1. In practice, fees, timing, rule differences and how much is actually available at that price often wipe out the gap. Gap Scanner shows you the numbers. It doesn't place trades or tell you what to do.
