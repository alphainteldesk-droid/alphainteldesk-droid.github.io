HOW IT WORKS

Say you buy a contract at 84¢. If you're right, it pays $1 when the market settles: your 84¢ back plus 16¢ profit. If you're wrong, it pays nothing and you lose the 84¢.

Each row shows the side to buy, the price now, your return after fees if you're right, and the close date, for Polymarket, Kalshi and Alpha Arcade side by side.

How this page works

How do I read a row?The bold line is the market. The line under it is the exact question for that contract, and whether you need the answer to be YES or NO. If it is, you get $1 per contract; if not, you lose what you paid.
What is "Price now"?The price you can actually buy at right now: the best ask for Yes, or 100¢ minus the best bid for No. Never the midpoint. We skip any contract whose bid and ask are more than 4¢ apart.
How is the return worked out?(100¢ − price − fee) ÷ (price + fee). The fee is the trading fee each site publishes for that exact market, read fresh every update. If a site doesn't publish one, we estimate it and mark it "est". Holding to settlement has no second fee. Kalshi rounds each order's fee up to the next cent, so a very small order (a few contracts) pays a little more than shown.
What is the DZ Grade?One letter for how safe a contract is to hold, from four checks (updated Nov 28, 2026, after our first 70 results). Liquidity: how many dollars of buy orders sit within 2¢ of the best bid for your side, i.e. how much you could sell right now (A = $10,000+, B = $2,000+, C = $300+, D = less; D contracts aren't listed, except Alpha Arcade's 10 busiest, which are usually thin: plan to hold those). Rules: how the result is decided (A = a clear named source like the BLS or the Federal Reserve, B = a named source with "credible reporting" as a backup, C = no source named, D = a judgement call). Type: 11 of our first 12 misses were count or range markets (tweet counts, post counts), price-path markets (how low will Bitcoin go), live TV votes, or one news story taking out several contracts. Count, range, price-path and live-vote markets get a D; scheduled official numbers (rate decisions, CPI, jobs) get an A; other event markets are neutral. Flip risk: the price's cushion above 50¢ compared with the swing we can expect before it settles (its 48-hour range, scaled to the days left, up to a month). A = 3x cushion or more, B = 2x, C = 1x, D = less; a price falling 5¢+ from its 48-hour high drops a letter. The grade is the average of the checks, rounded toward the weaker one; a D in Type or Flip risk caps it at C, and both make it D. It does not tell you whether the contract will pay out, and at an 85¢ price you need to be right 85% of the time just to break even.
What are EXIT, FLIP RISK and SAME STORY?EXIT: the dollars of buy orders on the order book for your side, i.e. how much you could sell back right now. The bright part counts orders within 2¢ of the best bid, the faint part within 5¢ (log scale up to $50K). ⚠ FLIP RISK: shown when the cushion is thin for how much the price moves (MEDIUM or HIGH), or when the price is falling (↓). The mini-chart is the last 48 hours: Polymarket and Kalshi from their own hourly history, Alpha Arcade from the last-trade prices our desk records every hour (thin Alpha markets can jump on one small trade). SAME STORY: other contracts that ride on the same news (the same country, person, company or coin). One headline can settle them all, so we list only the best one and show how many it stands for. Hover or tap to see the others.
How often are the rules checked?Polymarket and Kalshi rules are read fresh on every update, roughly every hour. Alpha Arcade rules are read once, when a market first appears on the site. All checks are automatic and can miss things. Rule clarifications and changes are rare, but they do happen, so always read the final rules on the market's own page before you trade.
How does the track record work?Every contract (Polymarket, Kalshi and Alpha Arcade) is logged the first time it appears on this page, at the price we first listed it. When it settles we record whether it paid out. Each contract counts once. One change so far: on Nov 21, 2026 we stopped listing weekly and daily crypto price contracts (a Bitcoin price on a single day is a coin flip on one week's swing) and took their results out of the record; the exact number removed is shown under the track record. Monthly and longer crypto markets still count. "If you'd bought $1 of each" is the average return after fees: a contract that pays out adds its return, one that doesn't loses the whole $1. Daily crypto and price markets are counted separately because they settle within days and would swamp the rest. Contracts a site cancels or settles 50-50 aren't counted. Past results don't predict future ones.
How do you keep the numbers right?Every update recomputes every row from scratch and throws out anything that doesn't add up. A watchdog checks the page after every update and alerts us if prices go stale, if a site stops publishing its fees, or if a fee rate changes. If prices are more than 2 hours old, this page tells you so.
What can go wrong?The outcome goes the other way and you lose the full price. Settlement can also be late or disputed, which ties your money up longer than the close date says. A very high return usually means the market sees real risk.
Where do Alpha Arcade prices come from?Straight from Alpha Arcade's order book on the Algorand blockchain: the price is the cheapest offer you could buy right now, same as Polymarket and Kalshi. If no one is selling at that moment, the row shows the last trade price marked "est". Alpha rows also need a trade in the last 5 days. Check the order book before you trade.
How are the 50 picked?We check hundreds of contracts every update and keep the best one per market. Each gets a score from its return after fees, how easy it is to sell, how clear its rules are (the two halves of the DZ Grade) and how much the market trades. The top 40 make the list, plus the 10 busiest Alpha Arcade contracts, so the site's Alpha coverage doesn't get crowded out by bigger sites. Contracts that are hard to sell (D) and daily crypto or price markets are left out.
What else is left out?Sports, thin markets, anything settling in under 6 hours or over 6 months, and returns under 0.3% after fees.
