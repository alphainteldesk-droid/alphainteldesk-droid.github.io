How this page works

How do I read a row?The bold line is the market. The line under it is the exact question for that contract, and whether you need the answer to be YES or NO. If it is, you get $1 per contract; if not, you lose what you paid.
What is "Price now"?The price you can actually buy at right now: the best ask for Yes, or 100¢ minus the best bid for No. Never the midpoint. We skip any contract whose bid and ask are more than 4¢ apart.
How is the return worked out?(100¢ − price − fee) ÷ (price + fee). The fee is the trading fee each site publishes for that exact market, read fresh every update. If a site doesn't publish one, we estimate it and mark it "est". Holding to settlement has no second fee. Kalshi rounds each order's fee up to the next cent, so a very small order (a few contracts) pays a little more than shown.
What is the DZ Grade?One letter for how clean a contract is to hold. It combines two things. Liquidity: how many dollars of buy orders sit on the order book within 2¢ of the best bid for your side, i.e. how much you could sell right now if you change your mind (A = $10,000+, B = $2,000+, C = $300+, D = less. D contracts aren't listed, except Alpha Arcade's 10 busiest: Alpha's order books are usually thin, so plan to hold those until they settle). Rules: how the result gets decided. A = a clear named source (like the BLS, the Federal Reserve or Rotten Tomatoes), B = a named source with "credible reporting" as a backup, C = no source named, D = a judgement call based on "credible reporting". The DZ Grade is the average of the two, rounded toward the weaker one. It does not tell you whether the contract will pay out. Alpha Arcade's order book is read straight from the Algorand blockchain.
How often are the rules checked?Polymarket and Kalshi rules are read fresh on every update, roughly every hour. Alpha Arcade rules are read once, when a market first appears on the site. All checks are automatic and can miss things. Rule clarifications and changes are rare, but they do happen, so always read the final rules on the market's own page before you trade.
How does the track record work?Every contract (Polymarket, Kalshi and Alpha Arcade) is logged the first time it appears on this page, at the price we first listed it. When it settles we record whether it paid out. Each contract counts once, and nothing is removed afterwards. "If you'd bought $1 of each" is the average return after fees: a contract that pays out adds its return, one that doesn't loses the whole $1. Daily crypto and price markets are counted separately because they settle within days and would swamp the rest. Contracts a site cancels or settles 50-50 aren't counted. Past results don't predict future ones.
How do you keep the numbers right?Every update recomputes every row from scratch and throws out anything that doesn't add up. A watchdog checks the page after every update and alerts us if prices go stale, if a site stops publishing its fees, or if a fee rate changes. If prices are more than 2 hours old, this page tells you so.
What can go wrong?The outcome goes the other way and you lose the full price. Settlement can also be late or disputed, which ties your money up longer than the close date says. A very high return usually means the market sees real risk.
Why "est" on Alpha Arcade rows?Alpha Arcade's live bid and ask aren't in our feed yet, so those rows use the last price and only appear if the market traded in the last 5 days. Check the order book before you trade.
How are the 50 picked?We check hundreds of contracts every update and keep the best one per market. Each gets a score from its return after fees, how easy it is to sell, how clear its rules are (the two halves of the DZ Grade) and how much the market trades. The top 40 make the list, plus the 10 busiest Alpha Arcade contracts, so the site's Alpha coverage doesn't get crowded out by bigger sites. Contracts that are hard to sell (D) and daily crypto or price markets are left out.
What else is left out?Sports, thin markets, anything settling in under 6 hours or over 6 months, and returns under 0.3% after fees.
